Earth Science Unit · Location: Colorado Springs

Where does the rain go?

A 2-week unit on how water moves between Earth's water sources (lakes, rivers, oceans, etc.) and the sky. Students will learn how that movement drives the weather outside the classroom window.

Designed by brgfx / Freepik

Unit Focus

  • The hydrosphere–atmosphere interaction.
  • How water moves between Earth's water and the air
  • How the water/air movement connects to our weather.

Two-Week Schedule

Three in-person days and two remote days each week.

  • In-person days carry anything hands-on or messy, labs, model building, group discussion.
  • Remote days carry content delivery and lower-stakes independent practice.

Water: Droughts, Floods, and Balance

Water Cycle Flashcards

A quick self-quiz on the four key terms — evaporation, condensation, precipitation, collection — good for review before the Day 4 check.

Passage of Water

An artistic, data-driven look at Earth's freshwater built with NASA satellite data — best as an "extend" resource for students who finish early.

The Planetary Engine of Earth's Water

A narrated audio overview that zooms out from the classroom water-cycle-in-a-bag to how the same cycle works at a whole-planet scale.

USGS National Water Dashboard

A live map of streamflow, precipitation, and water conditions — zoom in on Colorado to see real gauges near Colorado Springs update in real time.

Human Water Cycle

A TeachEngineering lesson on how people get, use, and dispose of water, and how engineers help conserve it — a good bridge to the "human" side of the cycle.

USGS Water Cycle for Kids

The USGS's kid-friendly water cycle diagram and explanations — a solid at-home reference for the Day 5 poster-labeling activity.

The Water Cycle Interactive Simulation

Drag clouds, trigger rain, and watch water move through evaporation, condensation, precipitation, and collection in real time.

Make a Thuderstorm!

Use this simulation to make a thunderstorm by adjusting the humidity and temperature.
